How to read a match

A thread is a set of pages one address edits in the same sittings, or pages with similar titles and sections. Each thread on a top-ten address is scored against every thread on every other address, across every year in the archive. A match then gets one of four explanations.

PoolThe two addresses hand the same page back and forth within the hour. One exit, several addresses.
RenumberingOne address goes quiet, another block starts, and pages from many threads carry over.
Parallel exitsThe same thread on two addresses in the same weeks, without enough handoffs to prove a pool.
Reappears laterThe thread stops on one address and turns up months or years later on another. The only one of the four that points at a person.
dashedA possible match: weaker score, or resting on a single shared page.

Method and limits

  • Two threads are compared on four things: shared pages weighted by rarity, title words, the section headings edited, and summary wording. Habits (hour of day, typical size of change, how often a summary is left) adjust the score.
  • A strong match needs a score of 0.60 or more and two independent kinds of evidence. A possible match needs 0.45 and at least a shared page, shared sections or shared summary wording. These cut-offs were set to recover cases already found by hand; there is no ground truth to test them against.
  • About half of all edits are one-off touches that belong to no thread and cannot be matched. The share for each address is shown beside it.
  • Known ways this goes wrong: live events and much-edited pages draw several people to the same place; given names in titles can collide; a renumbered network makes every thread look as if it moved.
  • Only metadata was used: titles, summaries, sizes and timestamps. No diff text was read. Email addresses and signatures in summaries are stripped. Nothing here identifies an editor.
