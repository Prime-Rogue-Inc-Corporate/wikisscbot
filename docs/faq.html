Frequently asked questions

What is Revision History?

Revision History is a public archive of edits made to Wikipedia, Wikidata, Wikimedia Commons, Wiktionary and Wikivoyage, without logging in, from IP addresses on Government of Canada networks. It currently holds 50,240 edits from 1,033 addresses, covering 2010 to 2025. It is published by Prime Rogue Inc. and built with an open collector called wikisscbot.

Where does the data come from?

From Wikipedia itself. Until November 2025, Wikimedia wikis published the IP address of anyone who edited without logging in. The collector asks the public MediaWiki API for every contribution from each Government of Canada address block, year by year. Nothing is scraped from private systems.

Which networks count as Government of Canada?

Two sources. The first is the public-domain list of department address ranges compiled for the @gccaedits bot in 2014–16, which covers Parliament, National Defence, the CBC and other departments. The second is every address prefix announced by Shared Services Canada's networks (AS2665, AS2675, AS2682, AS25689 and AS806), taken from RIPEstat routing records.

Does an edit from a government network mean the government made it?

No. It means the edit left through a government network. An edit from a government network is not evidence of who made it, or that it was made in an official capacity. Most addresses are shared gateways used by hundreds or thousands of people. The archive attributes edits to networks and never to named individuals.

Can you tell which department made an edit?

Sometimes. Labels such as House of Commons or National Defence come from ranges those institutions ran themselves. The label Shared Services Canada means the edit left through shared infrastructure that serves dozens of departments, so the department cannot be determined from the address alone.

Why does the archive stop in 2025?

On 4 November 2025 English Wikipedia replaced public IP attribution with temporary accounts, and other Wikimedia wikis followed. Logged-out edits made since then no longer show an IP address publicly. The archive ends on 3 November 2025 and does not try to reveal later addresses.

What was @gccaedits?

@gccaedits was a Twitter bot, launched in July 2014 by Nick Ruest, that tweeted each anonymous Wikipedia edit made from Government of Canada addresses. It was modelled on Ed Summers's @congressedits and drew wide press coverage in 2014. It stopped posting in April 2023. Revision History rebuilds the idea as a complete, searchable archive instead of a live feed.

How can I verify the data?

Every data pull writes a manifest listing the SHA-256 of each API response and each output file. Manifests are chained by hash, so altering an old run breaks every later link, and each one is timestamped in Bitcoin with OpenTimestamps. The site checks the files in your browser, and the same checks can be run with standard tools. The data page has the steps.

How complete is it?

Every configured year is re-collected in full on each run instead of being updated incrementally, and the collection status for each year is published. Edits that Wikipedia administrators have deleted or suppressed are not returned by the API and are not in the archive. Addresses outside the known ranges are not covered.

How should I cite it?

Cite the site name, the publisher and the date you accessed it, for example: Revision History (revisionhistory.ca), Prime Rogue Inc., accessed [date]. For a specific finding, also give the link to the Wikipedia diff, which is the primary source, and the manifest hash shown on the provenance page.

Is it legal to publish this?

The records are public. Wikipedia published each edit and its IP address under its own terms, and told logged-out editors that their address would be publicly visible. The archive reorganises that public record. This is general information, not legal advice.

How do I report an error?

Contact Prime Rogue Inc. through https://primerogueinc.com/. Corrections to a network label are recorded in the public change log.
