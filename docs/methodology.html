Methodology

How the 50,240 edits in the archive were collected, labelled and secured, and what the data cannot show.

Collection

For every Government of Canada IP block, the collector asks the MediaWiki API for all logged-out contributions in a calendar year (list=usercontribs with uciprange). It covers English and French Wikipedia, Wikidata, Wikimedia Commons, English Wiktionary and English Wikivoyage. Blocks are queried at /16 or smaller, and a block that times out is split into smaller blocks down to /24 until it succeeds. Every configured year is collected again in full on every run.

Network labels

Each address gets the label of the most specific range that contains it. Ranges come from the public-domain department list compiled for @gccaedits in 2014–16, and from every prefix announced by Shared Services Canada's networks (AS2665, AS2675, AS2682, AS25689, AS806) according to RIPEstat. An edit is attributed to a Shared Services Canada prefix only while that prefix was being announced by one of those networks, plus 90 days, because address space can change hands. A few individual addresses carry a label that Prime Rogue has confirmed with stronger evidence, such as reverse DNS; these are listed with their basis and start date in config/ranges_confirmed.json. A department label is a lead, not proof. The label "Shared Services Canada" means the department cannot be determined.

Topics

Pages are classified with Wikimedia's own article-topic model and with keyword groups called beats, tuned for Canadian government reporting. Beats are simple rules and will mislabel some pages.

Provenance

Each run writes a manifest with the code version, the parameters, the SHA-256 of every API response and every output file, and the hash of the previous manifest. The chain of manifests is timestamped in Bitcoin with OpenTimestamps. Published files are byte-for-byte copies, and the site re-checks their hashes in the browser. See data and verification.

Limits

  • An edit from a government network is not evidence of who made it, or that it was made in an official capacity. Most addresses are shared gateways used by hundreds or thousands of people.
  • English Wikipedia stopped publishing the IP addresses of logged-out editors on 4 November 2025. Later edits are out of scope, and nothing here tries to reveal them.
  • Edits that have been deleted or suppressed on Wikipedia are not returned by the API.
  • Address ranges change hands. A range that is a government network today may not have been one in an earlier year, and the reverse.
  • Edits made from home networks, mobile networks or VPNs by government staff are not captured.
