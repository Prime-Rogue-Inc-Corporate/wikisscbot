The foreign service's exit. For nearly a decade a single address named gate1.dfait-maeci.gc.ca carried almost every edit from the federal network to Canada's embassy, consulate and ambassador pages. It went quiet on 15 October 2020. Two weeks later a block of eight Shared Services Canada addresses picked up the same pages.

This profile describes network exit points, not people. A department gateway carries traffic for thousands of employees in Canada and abroad. An edit from one is not evidence of who made it, or that it was made in an official capacity. Counts are from this archive: every Wikimedia wiki, 2009 to 3 November 2025.

Registration and name

  • gate1: 198.103.104.0/24 is registered as DFAIT-MAECI to the Department of Foreign Affairs and International Trade, since 1993. The address 198.103.104.11 has public reverse DNS of gate1.dfait-maeci.gc.ca, and that name resolves back to the same address. The record still answered on 13 November 2026.
  • Successor: 198.103.198.0/24 is registered as GTIS-C1-198, the old common-government services allocation now under Shared Services Canada. None of its addresses has public reverse DNS.
  • Routing doesn't tell them apart. Both /24s have been announced by the same SSC networks on the same dates (AS2665 from 2018, AS2675 from September 2026).
  • Wikipedia's own label: volunteers tagged 198.103.104.11 "Dept. Foreign Affairs and International Trade", citing the gate1 host name. The successor addresses carry no label.

  • A clean break of 14 days. gate1's last edit was on 15 October 2020. The successor's first was on 29 October, from .248. No month has edits from both.
  • The successor's first weeks included Embassy of Canada, Moscow (23 November) and Trade Commissioner Service (26 November). gate1 had edited both.
  • Eight addresses, used in turn. The successor spreads its traffic over .248 to .255. Unlike DND's old pool, it never hands the same page between two addresses within the hour, so addresses seem to be assigned per session, not per request.
  • The block was otherwise new. None of the eight had edited before October 2020. They came online over the following eight months: .248 and .250 in late 2020, .252 to .254 in March–April 2021, and .251 in June 2021.

The diplomatic-mission pages

Edits to Canadian embassy, high-commission, consulate, ambassador-list, Trade Commissioner Service and Global Affairs Canada pages, by year, from any address in the archive:

  • 2011–2019: gate1 made 128 of 132 such edits.
  • 2020: one from gate1, then the first two from the successor block.
  • 2021–2025: the successor block made 29 of 33.

Confidence

Confirmed that 198.103.104.11 was a Foreign Affairs gateway: the registry assignment and a forward-confirmed host name agree. High that 198.103.198.248–.255 replaced it as the same department's Wikipedia-visible exit from October 2020. That rests on the two-week handover, the near-total shift of diplomatic-mission editing and page carry-over at about 15 times the control rate. Not established: whether the successor block serves Global Affairs Canada alone or is shared.
