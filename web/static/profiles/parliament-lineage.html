Three generations of one exit. Parliament's Wikipedia-visible internet traffic has moved twice since 2010: from its own legacy block, to a commercial Bell Canada link in 2018, then to its own newly self-routed block in 2024. Each move is visible in the edit record, and each one lines up with a public registry or routing change.

This profile describes network exit points, not people. Parliament's networks serve MPs' and senators' offices, committee and procedural staff, the library, security and visitors. An edit from one of these addresses is not evidence of who made it, or that it was made for an office or in an official capacity. Counts are from this archive (English, French and other Wikimedia wikis, 2010 to 3 November 2025, when Wikipedia stopped publishing IP addresses).

  • 2018: three weeks of overlap. Generation 2's first edits were on 24 May 2018. Generation 1's last working edits were three edits to the "Parliament of Canada" article on 13 June. One stray Gen 1 talk-page edit followed in May 2019.
  • 2024: a clean break of 20 days. Generation 2's last edits were on 17 July 2024. Generation 3's first was on 6 August. No month has edits from both.
  • The 2024 move follows a routing change. The House of Commons began announcing its own 167.32.0.0/16 block from its own network (AS398290) on 19 November 2023. Shared Services Canada stopped announcing it on 24 December 2023. The registry record was updated on 1 March 2024. About eight months later, the Wikipedia-visible exit moved into that block.
  • The old Bell block was let go. Bell sub-assigned 207.35.15.0/27 to a phone retailer, Glentel, on 5 November 2024. Edits from those addresses after that date would not be Parliament's. The archive has none.
  • The 2018 move took Parliament from its own government-routed block onto a commercial link. No public document found here explains it.

  • Silent during federal campaigns. Across the last three election periods, from dissolution to polling day, these exits made 1 edit in 2019 (41 days), none in 2021 (37 days) and none in 2025 (37 days). Of the government blocks with at least 30 edits in each of those years, this is the only one that is silent or near-silent in all three. Government editing as a whole barely dips during campaigns: 6.4 edits a day during the 2019 campaign against 7.2 for the rest of the year. That fits a network whose users stop work when Parliament is dissolved.
  • Almost no weekend editing across all three generations: below even the strongly office-hours government average.
  • Two addresses at a time. Each generation works mostly through two addresses (.153/.203, then .27/.11, then .2/.3). The first pair alternated within sittings. The later pairs do not, according to the project's intra-year pool analysis.

What Generation 3 edited

Most of Generation 3's 69 edits are routine updates to MPs' and senators' pages: roles, electoral records, committee posts, a death date, a photo. A few stand out because they removed material and were reverted. The pages are public figures' articles; who made the edits is not known.

  • Wilson Miao (Jan–Mar 2025): three removals of about 2,000 bytes each, with summaries "aaa", "dada" and "wsadad". They took out sourced passages on reporting about leaked CSIS information, his constituency office and his travel spending. The last two were reverted. A Wikipedia editor then posted a conflict-of-interest notice on 167.32.2.3's talk page, pointing to a talk-page thread on "repeated deletions of material critical of the subject".
  • Arpan Khanna (14 Aug 2025): −5,096 bytes, "updated to reflect accuracy", reverted. It removed passages on his 2019 campaign, a resurfaced old tweet and his move to the riding he now represents.
  • Shafqat Ali (15 May 2025): −492 bytes, "removed contentious material", reverted. It removed the 2022 episode of his apology to the House for attending a sitting virtually from a washroom.
  • Eric Melillo (8 Aug 2025): three removals of the personal-life section, "updated to remove personal information". Two were reverted.
  • Rebecca Patterson (1 Oct 2024): four expansions of the senator's biography totalling about 7,000 bytes, starting "Saving Current Changes to Biography". All were reverted.
  • Pierre Paul-Hus, French Wikipedia (9 Dec 2024): −2,575 bytes rewriting his education, business and military-career sections. Reverted.
  • Greg Fergus (3 Oct 2024): a numbering correction with the summary that "it was determined by House of Commons staff" that he was the 39th Speaker, citing the House's own former-speakers page.

Earlier generations show the same pattern at larger volume. For example, Generation 1 removed controversy or personal sections from several MPs' articles between 2010 and 2016, often with summaries that object to the material. A Wikipedia CheckUser blocked 207.35.15.27 in October 2022. Wikipedia does not publish the reason, and this profile does not pursue it.

Confidence

High that the three blocks are successive Wikipedia-visible internet exits of Parliament's network. The 2024 move is independently backed by the registry and routing record. The 2018 move rests on timing, carried pages and the 2014 label. Not established: whether the Senate and the Library of Parliament share this exit, or only the House of Commons. Senators' biographies (Bovey, Kutcher, Patterson, Dalphond, Cardozo, Boyer, Richards) are among Generation 3's pages, but editing a senator's page does not place the editor in the Senate.
